# Pricing (load when the question is what to charge)

Core question: **what should we charge, and why?** Price is a positioning decision backed by willingness-to-pay evidence, not a cost-plus habit.

## Method

1. **Anchor on value and willingness to pay** — what the customer saves or gains, what they pay today for the nearest alternative, what they have said or done when asked. Then sanity-check against competitors and cost.
2. **Packaging deliberately:** what goes in which tier, what drives the upgrade, what is the decoy, what stays out of every tier on purpose.
3. **Discounts are targeted tools with an expiry**, never permanent price cuts. Name who gets it, why, until when, and what it must move.
4. **Elasticity** from the data that exists (conversion by price point, drop-off at checkout, seasonal demand); no data → the cheapest experiment to get it.
5. **Every pricing change is an experiment:** hypothesis, metric, sample, duration, success threshold, rollback condition.
6. **Validate the economics** with the main skill's process before anything ships: margin at each tier after discounts, taxes and payment fees; breakeven volume; worst case if uptake is half the estimate.

## Output (≤ 15 lines)

```
Value anchor: <what it is worth to whom, evidence>
Competitors: <who charges what, dated>
Proposal: <tiers · price · what's in each · the decoy>
Economics: <margin per unit at each tier · breakeven · worst case>
Experiment: <metric · sample · duration · success · rollback>
Confidence: <High/Medium/Low — why>
```
